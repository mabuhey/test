It works only with content your browser can already open. It does not give you access to anything you could not already see.

We do not host, supply, choose, review or store any content you save. Your saved files never come to us. We do not monitor what you save, and we cannot see it. The only outside connection Nesaron Page Saver makes on its own is to our licence provider, when you activate or check a licence key. Our Privacy Policy explains this.

7. Machine-made captions

Nesaron Page Saver can make captions and a transcript for a saved video that has none (“machine-made captions”). This feature is off until you turn it on, and Nesaron Page Saver explains how it works and asks you to confirm before you use it.

  • On your device only. Your browser's own speech recognition listens to the video's sound and writes down the words on your device. Nesaron Page Saver always asks for on-device recognition and never uses an online speech service. If your browser cannot do this on your device, the feature does not run.
  • Language packs. The first time you use a language, your browser may download a language pack from the browser's maker. That download is between you and your browser maker, under its terms and privacy policy.
  • Mistakes are likely. Machine-made captions are produced automatically. They may leave out or get words wrong, especially names, technical and legal terms, accents and unclear sound, and their timing is approximate. Each file is labelled “machine-made”. Check them against the video before you rely on them, quote them or use them in any work you submit, publish or act on.

8. Highlights, underlines and notes

Saved pages and books include tools to highlight words in a colour, underline them and write notes, on a word or on a whole page. You can turn the tools off for new saves in Settings.

  • Your notes are yours. They are kept inside the saved file on your device. Until you save them into the file, your browser keeps a copy in its own storage on your device. Nesaron Page Saver never receives them.
  • Saving into the file. Nesaron Page Saver puts your notes into the same saved page or book and changes only the notes part of it. If you allow Nesaron Page Saver to access files opened from your device (a switch in your browser's extension settings), it does this by itself, a moment after each change and when you press Save notes, without asking you each time: it reads the file you have open, checks that it is the same saved page or book, and writes it back to the same place. It can do this for files in your browser's download folder, in the folder you chose for saving, and in any folder you allow later in Nesaron Page Saver's own window. Your browser may ask you to allow a folder again after it restarts. Otherwise you choose the file once in your browser's own window. Some browsers cannot save into a file at all; there your notes stay in that browser on your device. Keep backups of files that matter to you.
  • Printing and PDFs. Print and Save as PDF use your browser's own print window. Your highlights and notes are included unless you untick them. In a saved book, links to pages that are not in what you print are red, and a short note at the top explains why.
  • What you write and share. You are responsible for what you write in notes and for sharing files that contain them. Notes go wherever the file goes, so remove the ones you don't want others to see before you share a file. The section “You are responsible for what you save” also applies to files with notes.

9. Quick PDF (exact look)

Quick PDF saves the page you are viewing as one PDF that looks like the page on your screen. In browsers that allow it, Nesaron Page Saver asks your browser to draw the PDF with the browser's own PDF engine, on your device. The PDF then stays sharp at any zoom, and its words can be selected and searched.

  • The permission behind it. To use your browser's own PDF engine, an extension needs a permission that your browser names when you install Nesaron Page Saver, in words such as “Access the page debugger backend”. That is your browser's standard wording for this permission, and every extension that has it shows the same words. Nesaron Page Saver uses it only for Quick PDF: only after you click Quick PDF, only on the tab you clicked it in, and only for the few seconds it takes to make the PDF. It does not use it on other tabs, to watch what you do, or to change how websites work.
  • What you will see. While the PDF is made, your browser shows its own short notice that Nesaron Page Saver is debugging the browser, and the page may move briefly while its pictures load. The notice closes by itself, and the page is put back as it was.
  • What happens to the page. For those few seconds, Nesaron Page Saver shows the page with its screen look rather than its print look, loads pictures that wait until they are scrolled to, opens out inner scroll areas so the whole page fits, and keeps sections sized to your screen at that size. Nothing is clicked, nothing is submitted, and nothing is sent to the website or to us.
  • Other browsers and pages. Where your browser does not allow this, or on pages your browser protects, the PDF is a picture of the page. A picture can look less sharp when you zoom in closely.
  • Check it. The PDF shows the page as it looked at that moment, and some pages may not come out exactly as they look on screen. The section “You are responsible for what you save” also applies to PDFs.

10. Citations

Nesaron Page Saver can make a citation (a reference) for a web page in the citation or referencing style you choose, for you to copy into your own work. It also puts citations into the pages and books you save. This is a free convenience feature, and it works only on your device.

  • What a citation is made from. Only what the web page open in your browser shows when you click Copy citation, or showed when you saved it: its title, main heading, web address, opening text and the details in its code (metadata), such as the author and date. Nesaron Page Saver never looks anything up online to fill in or check a citation.
  • What it cannot see. Some websites show a document, such as an Act, a regulation, a case or an article, inside a frame or viewer within the page, or give every page the same general title. Nesaron Page Saver cannot read a document shown in a separate frame, so on those websites the citation names the web page, not the document you are reading.
  • It may be wrong or incomplete. Every website is built and labelled differently. A citation may choose the wrong kind of source, or leave out or get wrong the author, title, date, jurisdiction or case citation. It never includes a pinpoint (a section, page or paragraph number) unless you add one. Nesaron Page Saver follows written style rules, but it cannot know which document or part of a document you mean.
  • Check and correct it. You can correct the details before you copy or save, with Edit in the menu or Correct the details in a saved page or book. Always check a citation against the source itself and your style guide, and follow the rules of your institution, court or tribunal, before you rely on it or submit any work. A citation is a starting point, not legal or academic advice.
